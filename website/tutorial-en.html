1 An audible biome

The Aquorbium is not played note by note. It is a small sea in a glass sphere: five organisms live, move, perceive each other and sing. You play the environment - its desire, its water, its memory, the bodies of its creatures - and listen to what the life inside makes of it.

Nothing needs to be pressed for it to sound: with AUTONOMOUS BIOME on, it lives by itself. Your gestures change tendencies; the events come from the ecosystem.

Tip: Open LEARN and point at anything: each control explains itself in the panel below.

2 Every opening, another piece

Each time the Aquorbium opens, its seed draws a new world. A key and a mode; a harmonic language - functional, modal, Brazilian, impressionist or chromatic - that modulates and now and then lets the high voices wander into another key; who sings the bass, the middle voices and the melody; and an arc of tension of four to eight minutes, with its climax and its rest. The melody breathes after the cadences, another voice answers it with its motif transformed, and the motif comes back later.

The same seed also proposes a biome: how warm and how bright the water is, how murky, how strong the current, how much lives there, how dangerous it is. From it come which modules wake, each organism's body, the space, the ecology and the synthesis voices. The output stage - MASTER, CREATIVE MASTER, SUPPORT, LENS and ROOM - stays yours.

VARY, in the bottom strip, moves the controls slowly over the music's time, around where the seed or your hand left them: warmer and quicker at the climax, settling at rest. Move any control and it breathes around your value; the slider beside VARY sets how far the hand travels.

Tip: The status line shows the world - for example "B dorian · modal · orbit 16" - and the seed. A scene keeps the state you like; to hear an opening again, put its seed in session.fixedSeed.

3 The ecosystem

In the centre, the sphere: MORPH, GRAIN, MODAL, PULSAR and STRING. Each has a nature - predator, prey, migrant, sessile, territorial - and sees and hears only what the water lets through. Their positions move the sound in space.

The amber circle is the LENS: press and hold on the sphere and drag it to listen closer to a region; with Alt, behind the sphere.

Tip: The coloured lines between the organisms are their relations: coral is a hunt, amber a flight, cyan a school, pink a courtship.

4 Desire

The lower strip holds the desire of the ecosystem: DENSITY (how much happens) and DRIFT (how restless it is), and the MORPH between a neutral mix and the full ecological sound. ECO CONTROLS, inside the aquarium, opens cohesion, danger, the four listening modes and the scenes.

Desire alters tendencies; it never writes an event. Turn it slowly and give the biome time to answer.

Tip: Store a state you like in a scene (ECO CONTROLS → STORE) before exploring further.

5 The music of the ecosystem

In the GEOMETRY card, MUSIC makes the events come from the web of relations. A hunter imitates what it heard of its prey, in a canon whose delay is the travel of sound through the water; its echolocation clicks accelerate into a terminal buzz as it closes in. The prey flees in diminution, or falls silent. Rivals answer each other in hocket; allies sing together; a courting pair copies each other's song.

Compatible relations sound consonant, incompatible ones rub. The conflicts accumulate as tension, the harmony leans to the dominant, and when the conflict dissolves the music cadences.

Tip: Follow the panel under the sphere: the latest events appear there with their time - hunt, buzz, capture, cadence.

6 The water

The water has five states: ICE, COLD, TEMPERATE, TURBULENT and BOILING. They follow the season and the agitation of the organisms; WATER lets you warm or cool the aquarium. A change of state only happens after the latent heat is paid, so it is a moment of form, not a flicker.

Each state is a way of playing: ice is a crystal of discrete pitches, dry; cold water is viscous and legato; turbulence brings rubato and heterophony; boiling brings bubbles that ring at their own physical pitch. The water also absorbs the high frequencies: an organism far from the glass sounds darker.

Tip: The thin bar under the water states shows the latent heat already paid towards the next state.

7 Songs and memory

Every organism has a signature: a coda (a rhythm) and a whistle (a contour), like whales and dolphins. MEMORY decides whether a song is repeated - its identity - or developed: transposed, inverted, broken into cells. Allies that hear each other slowly copy units of each other's songs.

Now and then someone invents a new song, and those who hear it adopt it whole: a song revolution, as the humpbacks do across an ocean. When an organism leaves or is captured, the anchor keeps fragments of its song and returns them, slower and lower.

Tip: Low MEMORY for recognisable themes; high MEMORY for a music that keeps becoming something else.

8 Timbre: the bodies

In the TIMBRE card each organism can sing with a second body: voice (a formant song), matter, breath, string, drop, fold, membrane, glass, choir, bow or reed. MATTER moves the character inside the body - the vowels, the air, plucked to bowed, drops to rain - and MIX blends it with the native body.

ECOLOGICAL TIMBRE lets the ecosystem transform them: ice hardens, cold water softens, boiling sharpens; a hunter's body sharpens and comes forward, a prey's softens into breath, a courting pair opens its vowel.

Tip: The bodies are level-matched with the native ones: changing a body changes the colour, not the volume.

9 The synthesis device

Below the bodies, SYNTHESIS and EDIT open a synthesizer per organism. The ecosystem plays it: each note it sends opens the envelopes; you build the sound. At SYNTHESIS zero the instrument sounds exactly as before.

Each voice has two sources - oscillator, noise, the native body, FM, wavetable, additive, pulsar, string, matter or one of the bodies - then RING and FM between them, a filter, two envelopes and two LFOs. The three controls of a source change meaning with it; point at the source list to read what they do.

MODULATION is where the ecosystem enters the sound: eight routes from a signal - hunger, tension, distance, the SOFAR channel, the temperature of the water, the prey, the habitat, the room - to a parameter. A predator can open its filter when hungry; a prey can tremble when the reef is full.

PROPOSE draws a starting patch from the organism's species and strategy, and the instrument listens to it before offering it, so it is heard and levelled. The patches travel with the scenes.

Tip: Start with PROPOSE ALL and SYNTHESIS at half; then change one route at a time and listen to what the ecosystem does with it.

10 Lesson 1 - hearing the synthesis

Goal: hear what the synthesis device adds, without editing anything.

  1. In the TIMBRE card, bring SYNTHESIS to 0, then move it slowly to 1. At 0 you hear the organisms' own sound; at 1, only the synthesis.
  2. Click EDIT, then PROPOSE ALL. Listen for half a minute.
  3. Click PROPOSE ALL again, and again: each click is another starting point, drawn from each species' character.
  4. When one pleases you, stop. That is your starting patch.

The ecosystem plays the notes; you shape the sound. Nothing here needs to be played by hand.

Tip: Change one thing at a time and give it a few seconds: the ecosystem is slow.

11 Lesson 2 - the source and the brightness

Goal: two controls that change the most. Work on one organism only - choose MORPH at the top.

  1. SOURCE A: try oscillator, then FM, then string. The kind of sound changes completely.
  2. FILTER, CUTOFF: turn it left - the sound darkens, as if heard through more water; right - it opens and brightens.
  3. Leave SOURCE A on the one you liked and look for the CUTOFF where it sits best.

Only these two. The other controls can wait.

Tip: Point at the source list: its tip says what that source is and what its three controls do.

12 Lesson 3 - the gesture of the note

Goal: how each note begins and ends - the AMP ENVELOPE.

  1. ATTACK: short (to the left) and the note strikes; long and it swells in slowly, like a bow or a breath.
  2. RELEASE: short and the note stops dry; long and it lingers after the ecosystem lets it go.
  3. Compare two organisms: give GRAIN a short attack and a short release (a struck sound) and MODAL a slow attack and a long release (a resonance).

The same source, with another gesture, becomes another instrument.

Tip: Strings, matter and bodies are struck at every note: their own decay matters more than the envelope's.

13 Lesson 4 - colour and voice

Goal: the colour inside the sound.

  1. SHAPE, in SOURCE A: the source's main colour (its name changes with the source - ratio, position, tilt...). Turn it slowly.
  2. RESO, in the FILTER: more resonance makes the cutoff sing - a whistle at the edge of the sound.
  3. Now set the FILTER to formant (vowels). CUTOFF becomes TRACT (the size of a throat) and VOWEL walks A, E, I, O, U. Turn VOWEL slowly: the organism speaks.

TRACT low is a large, deep throat; high, a small, bright one.

Tip: High resonance near the notes can whistle: turn RESO back a little if it becomes sharp.

14 Lesson 5 - the ecosystem plays the sound

Goal: let the organisms' life move the sound - the MODULATION at the bottom. One route at a time.

  1. In the first free row choose energy, then cutoff, and an amount of about 0.4. Now the organism brightens when it is active and darkens when it rests.
  2. A second row: distance, cutoff, amount about -0.3. Farther from the centre, darker.
  3. For PULSAR try prey, then LFO 2 rate: when the reef is full of prey, its pulsing grows nervous.
  4. Liked it? COPY TO ALL gives this patch to every organism; store the whole state in a scene (ECO CONTROLS > STORE).

A route is a relation between the life of the biome and a parameter. Two or three are enough.

Tip: Point at a route's source box: its tip says what that signal of the ecosystem is.

15 Space, tail and master

SPACE places the sounds in a shared room of reflections; ENVIRONMENT adds a spectral signature and a bed of noise; TAIL is a late reverberation; MASTER holds the final level, the safety limiter and a creative master.

Each card has a MIX: at zero it is transparent, and the instrument sounds as it did before you touched it.

In the header, SPEAKERS / HEADPHONES: with headphones, each organism is heard from where it is in the sphere - left and right, front and back, above and below - through a model of a head.

Tip: OUTPUT TRIM is the volume; MUTE and PAUSE are in the header.

16 Life, populations and the place

Under the tension gauge, two bars: PREY and PREDATORS. They turn in slow cycles of a few minutes (Lotka-Volterra): plenty of prey fills the reef with events, the predators follow, the prey grow scarce, the reef thins, and it begins again.

Each voice lives: it eats (the grazers in proportion to how plentiful the prey are, the hunters only what they catch), every note costs it a little, it grows old - its sound darkens - and dies, of age or of hunger; its song goes to the reef, and after a silence the voice is born again singing its parents' songs recombined. The place has a SOUNDMARK, a motif born of the first song revolution, which returns after cadences and changes of the water. When more voices want to sing than the niche admits, the reef loses definition: the notes fuse into a mass (lo-fi).

Tip: Leave it running for ten minutes: you will hear abundance and scarcity, deaths and births, and the soundmark coming back.

17 The channel, the habitat, the room - and REC

The dashed band across the sphere is the SOFAR channel: the depth where sound is slowest, which holds it and carries it far (Munk). It rises towards the surface as the water cools. Organisms near it hear each other across the reef; a sound made in the channel does not reach a listener far above or below it - height decides who hears whom.

HABITAT is the reef listening to itself: how evenly its sound fills the bands and how alive it is in time (the ADI and ACI indices of ecoacoustics). A healthy habitat holds more events. ROOM, next to SEA, is optional: the microphone becomes the reef's hearing; the voices rise over the room's noise and leave its register. The aquarium's own sound coming back through the speakers is discounted; only a level and a register are measured, nothing is recorded or sent.

REC, in the header, records what you hear to Music/Aquorbium/<date>/ - a 32-bit float WAV (or 24-bit PCM with dither, recording.format = pcm24), with a text beside it: loudness (LUFS, loudness range), true peak, the session's seed and the controls.

Tip: Each session has its own seed, shown in the audio status: to hear a session again, put its seed in session.fixedSeed.

18 Scenes, pause and languages

Scenes keep every control: store, recall, rename, import and export them in ECO CONTROLS. MUTE closes the sound while the ecosystem keeps living; PAUSE stops its time, and RESUME continues from there.

The EN/PT/FR/ES button changes the language of the whole interface, this tutorial and LEARN. AUDIO chooses the output; if you hear clicks, 48 kHz with 512 samples is a safe start.

Tip: Everything in the Aquorbium is slow on purpose: give each gesture a minute.
